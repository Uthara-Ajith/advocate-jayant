Bail in Criminal Cases: What Accused Persons Should Know

Bail is an important part of criminal procedure. A person facing criminal allegations may have questions about arrest, custody and the possibility of obtaining release on bail.

The applicable procedure depends on the nature of the alleged offence and the circumstances of the case.

What Is Bail?

Bail generally refers to the release of an accused person from custody subject to conditions imposed under the applicable law or by the court. Section 2(b) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita defines bail as the release of a person accused of or suspected of commission of an offence from the custody of law upon certain conditions imposed by an officer or court on execution by such person of a bond or a bail bond.

Bail does not mean that the person has been found innocent or guilty.

Anticipatory Bail

In appropriate circumstances, a person who apprehends arrest may seek anticipatory bail under the applicable law. Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 deals with the direction for grant of bail to a person apprehending arrest. A person apprehending arrest on accusation of having committed a non-bailable offence can apply for anticipatory bail before the High Court or the Court of Session.

Whether such relief is available depends on the allegations, circumstances and statutory requirements.

Regular Bail

A person who has been arrested may, depending on the circumstances and applicable law, seek regular bail from the appropriate court.

Why Legal Representation Is Important

Bail proceedings can involve questions concerning the allegations, investigation, custody, statutory provisions and conditions that may be imposed.

A criminal lawyer can examine the available information and advise on the appropriate legal procedure.

What Documents May Be Relevant?

Depending on the case, documents and information concerning the accused, the allegations, previous proceedings and other relevant circumstances may be important.
